## 1. Executive summary

Woodpeckers is presented on Instagram as **Woodpeckers Sneakers and
Streetwear**, a Ghana-based retailer positioning itself around
premium-quality and exclusive sneakers and streetwear, with the phrase
**"Affordable Luxury, Bold & Innovative."**

The opportunity identified for this project is not simply to build a
conventional online shop. Woodpeckers has communicated a campaign called
**Meet the Reserve**, in which customers can make offers on selected
products. A carefully designed website could bring product discovery,
offer submission, offer review, customer updates and purchase completion
into one organized experience.

The website is currently a **speculative concept to pitch to the
business**. The business has not, based on the information available in
the project brief, commissioned the work, approved the proposed
features, supplied official assets, confirmed its operational policies,
or agreed to a commercial arrangement. The concept must never be
represented as the official Woodpeckers website without authorization.

### Core project thesis

> Build a premium, mobile-first sneaker and streetwear storefront that
> complements Woodpeckers' social channels and turns Meet the Reserve
> into a clear, trackable digital customer journey.

The proposed website should support: - Product discovery and category
browsing. - Product details, sizes and variants. - Cart, checkout and
order management. - A dedicated Meet the Reserve experience. - Customer
accounts and offer history. - Product, inventory, order and offer
administration. - Ghana-appropriate checkout and payment options,
subject to business approval.

## 7. The Meet the Reserve --- product opportunity and functional concept

This should be the signature feature of the website and the centrepiece
of the client pitch.

### Proposed customer journey

1.  Customer opens the Meet the Reserve page.
2.  Customer reads a short explanation.
3.  Customer browses products that are eligible for offers.
4.  Customer opens a product.
5.  Customer selects size / variant.
6.  Customer enters an offer amount and contact details.
7.  The system validates and records the offer.
8.  Customer receives a unique reference.
9.  Customer can check the offer status.
10. Staff review the offer or the system evaluates it using approved
    rules.
11. Customer receives the outcome.
12. An accepted offer proceeds to the approved purchase and payment
    flow.

### Possible offer statuses

-   Submitted.
-   Under review.
-   Accepted.
-   Counteroffer sent.
-   Counteroffer accepted.
-   Rejected.
-   Expired.
-   Withdrawn.
-   Awaiting payment.
-   Completed.

### Possible administrator tools

-   Offer queue.
-   Offer details.
-   Customer contact details.
-   Product and size.
-   Offered amount.
-   Internal reserve configuration.
-   Accept / reject / counteroffer.
-   Internal notes.
-   Expiry controls.
-   Offer history / audit trail.
-   Link to resulting order and payment.

### Business rules that must be confirmed

-   Is the reserve a fixed amount for each product?
-   Is the reserve amount hidden from customers?
-   Does the customer name any amount?
-   Is the offer manually reviewed?
-   Is automatic acceptance ever used?
-   Can staff counteroffer?
-   Can customers revise or withdraw offers?
-   Can multiple customers offer on the same product?
-   Does the first qualifying offer win?
-   Does an accepted offer lock the selected size?
-   How long does the customer have to pay?
-   Is a deposit required?
-   What happens if payment is not completed?
-   Does the offer expire?
-   How is the final price communicated?
-   Does the campaign apply to all products or selected products only?

Do not invent Woodpeckers' answers. Build a configurable demonstration
flow until the business confirms its policy.
